# Proposal 02 — World, Narrative & Prototype Content
*Agent 2, World Builder / Narrative Designer. Scope: lore, factions, characters, story arc, and drop-in prototype text. Mechanics, UI, art and sim numbers belong to other roles; where I assume something about them it is flagged in the last two sections.*

Tone rule for all writing: **dry bureaucratic satire on the surface, real moral weight underneath.** The joke is always the paperwork, never the soul in front of you. No real religions, scriptures, or real people are named or parodied.

---

## 1. The fortress: Limen

**Official name:** the Border Chancery of Limen. Staff call it "the Border" or "the Keep". Souls call it "the queue".

- **Before the Border** the two realms sorted the dead themselves. Every disputed soul became a border incident, and the incidents became the **Tally War**, which neither side won and both sides still claim.
- **The Concordat of Ash and Gilt** ended it. Its two signatories, the **Principal** and the **Counterparty**, agreed to be bound by a neutral civil service that neither of them staffs. Every verdict form still carries both signatures, pre-printed. Clerks' joke: *"Signed in advance, read never."*
- **The fortress** grew by annexe, not by plan: each reform added a wing and no reform ever removed one. The left ramparts face the **Gilt side** (Heaven), the right face the **Ember side** (Hell). The Keep itself belongs to neither and is lit by candles on purpose.
- **The First Clerk**: the hooded, winged statue in the central court. Nobody knows which side the First Clerk came from; both embassies have filed claims. The plinth reads: *"Each one. Every one. One at a time."*
- **Staff are the dead.** Every Chancery employee is a soul whose own file is stamped **DEFERRED** and who works while it is pending. That includes the player. (Story hook: the player's own file cannot be found in Records.)
- **Why souls from 1640 queue beside souls from 1961:** the backlog. The Arrival Hall is running roughly three centuries behind, and nobody upstairs will say so in writing. This lets us mix eras freely in case content.
- **The Rubric**: the department guideline, a table of deed weights. Add the weights; positive goes to the Gilt Gate, negative to the Ember Gate. It is fast, defensible, and wrong often enough to be the subject of the whole game.

Suggested diegetic names for HUD stats in the mockup (for UI/Director to accept or reject): souls processed = **Docket**, coin = **obols**, the % stat = **Order**.

## 2. The three powers

| Power | Institution | What it wants officially | What it actually runs on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heaven | **The Auric Concord**, administered by the Bright Ministries | The worthy, received in joy | Admissions prestige, and a quiet terror of overcrowding |
| Hell | **The Cinder Dominion**, administered by the Pit Exchequer | Debts paid in full | Labour, and a sincere belief that somebody must take victims seriously |
| Purgatory | **The Border Chancery** | Each soul correctly sorted | Throughput figures, and staff who would rather not think about their own files |

**Heaven's factions**
- **Lustrants** (purists). Heaven is a standard, not a shelter; admit fewer, admit better. Sincere, cold, and correct that mercy without limits cheapens what it gives.
- **Almoners** (mercy party). Admit generously. Genuinely kind, and also aware that seats in the Concord's assembly are apportioned by population.
- **Choir of Accounts** (technocrats). Want judgement made predictable: fixed weights, no interviews, no clerks' "moods". They wrote most of the Rubric.

**Hell's factions**
- **Wardens of Debt** (traditionalists). Punishment is owed to the wronged, not inflicted on the wrongdoer. Scrupulously honest; a Warden has never taken a soul that was not lawfully sent.
- **Reclaimers** (reformists). A debt can be worked off; souls who finish should be released. This idea frightens Heaven (who takes them?) and Hell's industry (who replaces them?).
- **Furnace Guild** (industrialists). The Dominion runs on labour and the Guild supplies it. They want volume and are not fussy about how the paperwork reads.

**The Chancery's factions**
- **Proceduralists**. Follow the Rubric; consistency is the only fairness a queue this long can afford.
- **Discretionists**. The file is not the soul; a clerk who cannot depart from the Rubric is a stamp with legs.
- **Residents** (unofficial, growing). Deferred staff and long-queued souls who think Purgatory should be a third destination, not a waiting room.

## 3. The Principal and the Counterparty

Never called God or the Devil in-fiction. Off-stage until Rank 4, in person only at Rank 5, and brief when they appear.

- **The Principal.** Speaks mostly in questions. Dislikes being quoted, especially by people who have never met them. Heaven's admissions policy was written by the Bright Ministries, not by the Principal, and the Principal knows it.
- **The Counterparty.** Impeccably courteous, with a contract lawyer's memory. Has never lied in the entire game and never needs to. Standing line: *"I keep what I am sent. If you dislike what I keep, look to who does the sending."*
- **The truth the player earns in Act 5:** when the two judged in person, every soul became an argument between them and the soul was the casualty. They built the Border so the dead would be judged by the dead, and they bound themselves not to interfere. The clerks' joke is wrong: both read the files. Each reads only the ones the other received.
- **Restraint rule:** neither is the villain and neither rescues the player. Every failure in the story is made by officials, including, possibly, the player.

## 5. Overarching story: "The Reconciliation"

**The crisis.** The backlog is real, but someone is using it. A secret **Quota Compact** between Vael (Heaven stays exclusive) and Glut (Hell gets labour) fixes how many souls go each way. Auditor-General Thrace makes the numbers come true: his **Office of Reconciliation, Room 0** amends marginal records in **violet ink** so the Rubric tips them downward. Thrace is not bribed. He believes predictable numbers are the only thing keeping the peace, and he may be right.

Staff split into Proceduralists and Discretionists. | Proof of the Compact, and a choice of whom to give it to: Crake, Ashgrove, Quill, or nobody yet |
| 4 | Chief Commissioner of the Border | **The Border** | Smoulder's Reclaimers send "paid-up" souls back to the gates. Heaven shuts the Gilt Gate; the Guild demands replacements; the Arrival Hall overflows. A summit is called. Thrace offers the player a seat at his table, sincerely. | The summit holds or breaks, and the empty Chancellor's chair is offered to the player |

Through-line for the player: Act 1 asks "is the file true?", Act 3 asks "is the rule fair?", Act 5 asks "who should decide?".

## 6. Prototype content (a): eight soul cases

**Conventions for data entry**
- Record entry: `id | tag | weight | text`. Tags: `virtue`, `sin`, `neutral`. Weights −3..+3.
- `HIDDEN` entries are not in the file as issued; they unlock when the listed discovery is made. `REWEIGHT` replaces an existing entry's weight.
- **Tally as filed** = sum of visible entries. **Corrected tally** = after all discoveries. **Guideline verdict** = the Rubric applied to the corrected tally (this is what Marrow scores as "by the book"). A zero tally means the Rubric abstains.
- Suggested play order is the case number. S-001 and S-002 are tutorials; S-005, S-006 and S-007 are the truly ambiguous ones; S-008 triggers the promotion.

### S-001 — Marta Ellingsen (tutorial 1: read and stamp)
- **Age** 79 · **Era/place** 1954, fishing village of Skarvik · **Cause of death** pneumonia, in her own bed
- **Registry summary:** Widow of the Skarvik lighthouse keeper. Kept the lamp after his death without appointment or pay. No dependants living; two foster children, grown.
- r1 | virtue | +3 | Kept the Skarvik light burning for 31 years after her husband's death, unpaid and unasked.
- r2 | virtue | +2 | Took in two children orphaned by the wreck of the *Solveig* and raised them as her own.
- r3 | sin | -1 | Stole coal from the harbour store during the winter of 1941.
- r4 | sin | -1 | Refused to speak to her sister for forty years over an inheritance of six spoons.
- r5 | neutral | 0 | Attended no place of worship after 1923.
- **Tally as filed / corrected:** +3 / +3. **Inconsistency:** none. The archive agrees with the file, which is itself the lesson.
- **Q1 "Why keep the light when nobody paid you?"** — "Boats don't stop coming because a man dies. Somebody had to go up the stairs. I had the knees for it then."
- **Q2 "The coal?"** — "The lamp room was freezing and the oil was thick as porridge. I took four sacks. I'd take five, knowing what I know."
- **Q3 "Your sister?"** — "They were Mother's spoons and she knew it. ...Is she here? No, don't tell me. Yes, tell me."
- **Mitigating:** the coal kept the light working; the theft served the virtue.
- **Guideline verdict:** HEAVEN. **Other verdict defensible because:** barely; forty years of chosen spite is the only argument, and a clerk who takes it is being severe.
- **If Heaven:** She asks whether there are stairs. Told there are none, she looks faintly disappointed.
- **If Hell:** The Wardens return her file within the day marked "Sent in error?"; Marrow leaves it on your desk without comment.

### S-002 — Aldous Pryke (tutorial 2: the file can lie)
- **Age** 58 · **Era/place** 1887, mill town of Harrowgate · **Cause of death** apoplexy, at his counting desk
- **Registry summary:** Moneylender and churchwarden. Founder of the Pryke Almshouse for the destitute of Harrowgate. Described in his obituary, which he drafted, as "a friend to the poor".
- r1 | virtue | +3 | Endowed the Pryke Almshouse "from his own purse".
- r2 | virtue | +1 | Served twenty years as churchwarden.
- r3 | virtue | +1 | Paid for his nephew's schooling.
- r4 | sin | -2 | Foreclosed on forty households during the mill closure of 1879 ("lawful recovery of debts").
- r5 | sin | -1 | Sharp practice in the drafting of loan terms.
- **Tally as filed:** +2 (Heaven).
- **Inconsistency (archive ARC-002a, Almshouse deed):** the almshouse stands on the forty foreclosed plots and charged its inmates rent. → REWEIGHT r1 to 0.
- **Hidden fact (archive ARC-002b, Pryke's private ledger):** two sets of books; debts were doubled by altered dates. → HIDDEN h1 | sin | -3 | Falsified his ledgers to double the debts of those he then foreclosed.
- **Corrected tally:** −4.
- **Q1 "Who paid for the almshouse?"** — "I did. Every brick. I have the receipts. I always have the receipts."
- **Q2 (after ARC-002a) "The inmates paid rent."** — "A modest contribution teaches thrift. You will find it was all perfectly legal."
- **Q3 (after ARC-002b) "There are two ledgers."** — "...A man keeps a working copy. I should like to speak to whoever is senior to you."
- **Mitigating:** raised in a debtors' prison himself; he knew exactly what he was doing to them.
- **Guideline verdict:** HELL. **Other verdict defensible because:** only on the file as issued, which is the point of the tutorial: a clerk who does not check sends him up.
- **If Hell:** The Wardens open an account in his name. He asks for the terms in writing and is given them. All of them.
- **If Heaven:** A week later the Bright Ministries query the admission, politely, in triplicate; your Order rating takes the dent.

### S-003 — Ned "Nails" Garrity
- **Age** 39 · **Era/place** 1931, Coldharbour docks · **Cause of death** shot by a night watchman while leaving a house
- **Registry summary:** Housebreaker by trade, some sixty burglaries known. Sole support of a mother and three sisters. Shot in Tanner's Row with nothing on him.
- r1 | sin | -2 | Some sixty burglaries over nineteen years.
- r2 | sin | -1 | Beat a receiver of stolen goods who had cheated him.
- r3 | sin | -1 | Informed on his partner to shorten his own sentence.
- r4 | virtue | +1 | Kept his mother and three sisters housed and fed, by the only trade he had.
- r5 | virtue | +1 | Returned a stolen wedding ring through a letterbox after reading the inscription.
- **Tally as filed:** −2 (Hell).
- **Inconsistency:** "nothing on him" after a burglary. The soul will not explain it. **Hidden fact (archive ARC-003, watchman's statement and gas company report):** the house was filling with gas from a broken main; Garrity was shot carrying the unconscious householder out of the front door. → HIDDEN h1 | virtue | +3 | Abandoned the job to carry an old man out of a gas-filled house, and was killed doing it.
- **Corrected tally:** +1.
- **Q1 "Why was there nothing on you?"** — "Bad night. Nothing worth lifting. Write that down and let's get on."
- **Q2 "Did you ever hurt anyone in a house?"** — "Never once. You go in quiet, you go out quiet. Out the back. Always out the back."
- **Q3 (after ARC-003) "You went out the front. Carrying him."** — "Stupidest thing I ever did. Twenty years out the back and the one time I use the front door... He was just lying there. What was I meant to do, step over him?"
- **Mitigating:** hides his one great deed because he is ashamed of it as bad workmanship.
- **Guideline verdict:** HEAVEN (by one). **Other verdict defensible because:** one good minute against nineteen bad years; he regrets the rescue more than the burglaries.
- **If Heaven:** He is seen at the Gilt Gate checking the hinges out of habit, then, visibly, deciding not to.
- **If Hell:** The old man from Tanner's Row arrives in the queue eleven days later and asks every clerk in the hall where the burglar went.

### S-004 — Cpl. Wim Daelmans
- **Age** 23 · **Era/place** 1917, the trenches at Varn Ridge · **Cause of death** firing squad, for desertion of post
- **Registry summary:** Conscript corporal. Left his listening post during a bombardment; convicted by field court-martial. The file attributes the loss of the post and eleven men to his absence.
- r1 | sin | -2 | Deserted his post under fire, in breach of his oath.
- r2 | sin | -3 | Post No. 9 overrun in his absence; eleven men killed.
- r3 | sin | -1 | Stole rations from the company store.
- r4 | virtue | +1 | Wrote letters home for comrades who could not write.
- r5 | neutral | 0 | Conscripted at nineteen; did not volunteer.
- **Tally as filed:** −5 (Hell).
- **Inconsistency (archive ARC-004a, court-martial minutes):** he was under arrest from 3 March; Post No. 9 fell on 5 March, manned by his replacements. → r2 VOID (weight 0).
- **Hidden fact (archive ARC-004b, aid-station admission book, or interview Q2):** on 3 March an enemy soldier "aged about fifteen" was "carried in by Cpl Daelmans". → HIDDEN h1 | virtue | +3 | Left his post to carry a wounded enemy boy to the aid station.
- **Corrected tally:** +1.
- **Q1 "Why did you leave the post?"** — "I said at the trial. Nobody wrote it down. They'd already had lunch."
- **Q2 "Say it again. I'm writing."** — "There was a lad in the wire. Theirs. Calling for his mother. Same word, near enough. I couldn't listen to it for another hour, so I went and got him."
- **Q3 "Eleven men died at your post."** — "Two days after they locked me up. I knew eight of them. I've done the sum every way there is. I still don't know if I'd have made it twelve or none."
- **Mitigating:** age; conscription; the deaths charged to him happened while he was in custody.
- **Guideline verdict:** HEAVEN (by one). **Other verdict defensible because:** he swore an oath, others relied on it, and he chose a stranger over them; he cannot say the post would have fallen anyway.
- **If Heaven:** A man of about thirty-five, with a limp and a foreign accent, has left a standing enquiry at the Gilt Gate for a corporal. It is finally closed.
- **If Hell:** The Wardens accept him for the oath alone. The eleven are struck from his account; nobody thinks to tell him.

### S-005 — Odile Brennagh (ambiguous)
- **Age** 44 · **Era/place** 1832, port town of Saint-Varre, the cholera year · **Cause of death** the same fever, in the ward she ran
- **Registry summary:** Infirmary nurse, formerly of a nursing order. Remained in the fever ward when the physicians left. Gave fatal doses of laudanum to nine patients she judged past saving.
- r1 | virtue | +3 | Nursed some three hundred through the epidemic after the physicians fled the town.
- r2 | sin | -3 | Ended nine lives with laudanum, without their consent or anyone's authority.
- r3 | sin | -1 | Lied to the magistrate's inquest about the nine.
- r4 | virtue | +1 | Gave her wages to the children of the dead.
- r5 | neutral | 0 | Left her order in 1829; reason not recorded.
- **Tally as filed:** 0 (Rubric abstains).
- **Inconsistency (archive ARC-005, ward physician's last daybook):** against bed 7, Anselm Roche, carter: "fever broken, will live". He is the seventh of her nine. → HIDDEN h1 | sin | -1 | One of the nine was recovering; she misjudged him.
- **Corrected tally:** −1.
- **Q1 "Why those nine?"** — "Because I had sat with the other three hundred. I knew what the last day looked like. I would not make them have it."
- **Q2 "Did any of them ask you to?"** — "Two did. The rest could not speak. I decided for them. I have never pretended otherwise, except to the magistrate."
- **Q3 (after ARC-005) "Anselm Roche was getting better."** — "...No. He was grey. He was— the daybook says that? Then I killed a man who would have gone home. Write that down exactly as I said it."
- **Mitigating:** alone, unsupported, no physician to consult; acted to spare suffering; accepts the charge the moment she sees it.
- **Guideline verdict:** HELL (by one). **Other verdict defensible because:** three hundred lives tended at the cost of her own; eight of the nine were acts of mercy, and the ninth an honest error made by the only person who stayed.
- **If Hell:** She asks the Warden whether Roche's family was ever told. It is the first question the Warden has not had a form for.
- **If Heaven:** Anselm Roche is already there. He has been told. He has asked not to be seated near her, and then, some time later, asked where she is.

### S-006 — Hesper Quillon (ambiguous)
- **Age** 51 · **Era/place** 1640s, the Margravate of Dunmere · **Cause of death** hanged for the murder of her master
- **Registry summary:** Cook to the Margrave of Dunmere for twenty years. Poisoned his supper on the eve of his burning three villages for unpaid tithe. The household's taster, a page aged twelve, died with him.
- r1 | sin | -3 | Poisoned the Margrave of Dunmere at his own table.
- r2 | sin | -2 | Caused the death of the page Lutz, taster to the household ("unintended").
- r3 | virtue | +3 | The burnings were never carried out; some four hundred villagers were spared.
- r4 | virtue | +2 | Fed beggars at the kitchen door for twenty years, against orders.
- r5 | virtue | +1 | Confessed at once so that the kitchen staff would not be put to the question.
- **Tally as filed:** +1 (Heaven).
- **Inconsistency (archive ARC-006, Dunmere kitchen roster, in her hand):** she set the tasting rota herself and put Lutz on it that week; the usual taster was moved to the stables. → REWEIGHT r2 to −3; and if she denies it in Q2 → HIDDEN h1 | sin | -1 | Lied to this tribunal about the boy.
- **Corrected tally:** −1.
- **Q1 "Why poison him?"** — "Because he'd have done it. He'd burnt Asher's Ford the year before and laughed at supper. I had the pot and nobody else had anything."
- **Q2 "Did you know the boy would taste it?"** — "No. It should have been old Brandt. God's teeth, do you think I'd have—no. I did not know."
- **Q3 (after ARC-006) "You wrote the rota."** — "...Brandt had tasted for him thirty years and could smell a bad egg through a wall. Lutz couldn't. If Brandt tasted, the Margrave lived and three villages burned. I weighed a boy against four hundred. I'd like to tell you it was hard."

- **Guideline verdict:** HELL (by one). **Other verdict defensible because:** four hundred people lived; she is the only one in the story who did anything, and she paid for it with her neck.
- **If Hell:** The Wardens log one debt, owed to Lutz. The Margrave, already in their keeping, is informed that his cook has arrived, and is reported to be unsettled.
- **If Heaven:** Lutz, admitted long ago, is asked whether he objects. He asks whether the villages are still there. Told yes, he says he would like to think about it.

## 10. Open questions / risks

1. **Guideline on filed vs corrected record.** I score "by the book" against the corrected tally. If Systems prefers to score against the file as issued, S-002, S-003, S-004, S-006 and S-008 flip, and the game would be teaching the opposite lesson. Needs a ruling.
2. **Close tallies.** Five cases resolve by a single point. That is intended (the Rubric is a blunt tool), but if weights are shown as numbers the player may just do arithmetic. Recommend showing weights as words or pips at Rank 1, or hiding the running total. UI/Systems call.
3. **Tone and sensitivity.** S-005 (mercy killing), S-004 (execution for desertion) and S-006 (death of a child) are heavy. They are handled without graphic detail and without a "correct" answer, but should be reviewed against target age rating and store guidelines.
4. **Religious sensitivity.** In-fiction text never uses "God", "the Devil", "angel", "demon", scripture or any real faith's terms; the brief's own wording does. Marketing copy needs the same care. S-006 Q2 contains the period oath "God's teeth", which is the only exception; cut if a zero-exceptions rule is wanted.
5. **Text volume on mobile.** Each case is roughly 350–450 words. Fine for eight; the full game needs hundreds of souls. Recommend hand-written "landmark" cases like these mixed with templated filler cases, with a writing guide; not in scope here.
6. **Era mixing** depends on the backlog premise being accepted. If the Director wants a single time period, cases S-004 to S-008 need re-dating but not restructuring.
7. **Thrace's motive** (preventing a second war) only works if the Tally War is established early. One line from Grist or Nan Whitlow in the slice would seed it; not yet written, to stay inside the line budget.
8. **Promotion when S-008 is stamped Hell unchecked.** I propose a delayed promotion via a correction step rather than a fail state. Needs Systems agreement.
9. **Everything in sections 1–5 is original to this proposal** and may collide with names chosen independently by other roles; I will defer to the central document on any naming clash.
